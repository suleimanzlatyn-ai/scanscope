INSPECTION METHOD

Remote Visual Inspection

Remote visual inspection uses suitable viewing equipment such as a videoscope or camera system to observe areas that may be difficult to reach directly. Feasibility depends on access, geometry and available equipment.

Where remote viewing may help

01

Restricted openings

A remote camera may view selected internal spaces through an available opening when field of view and working distance are suitable.

02

Internal condition checks

Images or video can document visible deposits, damage or condition indicators, but only within the observable area.

03

Reduced direct access

Remote viewing may reduce the need for direct visual access in some situations but does not remove all safety requirements.

SCANSCOPE TECHNOLOGIES

What to confirm

  • Opening size, access route, depth and geometry
  • Expected field of view and condition of interest
  • Lighting, cleanliness, isolation and safety needs
  • Whether still images, video or written observations are required
  • Equipment availability and achievable image quality
SCANSCOPE TECHNOLOGIES

Scope note

Remote visual inspection can only report what is visible with the selected equipment and conditions. Confirm feasibility, availability and limitations before relying on the method for a critical decision.
